Start where you actually are

You almost never know which prompt you want — you know what you have to produce by Friday. So pick whichever of these matches what is in your head right now:

  • I know the artefact I need (acceptance criteria, a KPI table, a cutover plan) → search the prompts.
  • I know my project type but not the artefact (agile delivery, procurement, a data warehouse) → start from a delivery context.
  • I have an AI assistant open right now → give it the router skill and just describe the work.

What makes these different from a list of prompts

They are scored, and the scoring is honest about itself
Ten criteria, 0–2 each. Six can be checked by machine and are; four need judgement and are labelled as such. The library averages 19.67/20 — and the scoring page explains at length why that number is weaker evidence than one person saying it worked.
They carry safety rules you would otherwise forget
Every prompt tells the model to stop if you paste real personal data, forbids invented facts, and gives a flagging convention — [TBC] for unverified, [PROPOSED — VALIDATE] for anything it made up itself.
They are stored as fields, not as text
Which is why any prompt can be rebuilt as CO-STAR, RISEN, TIDD-EC or six others, or turned into an agent skill — and why the site can tell you what each conversion costs you.
They are organisation-neutral
No client names, no sector assumptions, no jurisdiction-specific rules. Two edits make them yours: add your organisation to the context, and swap each worked example for one from your own domain.
