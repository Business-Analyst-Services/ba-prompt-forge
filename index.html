AI prompts for business analysts

Ready-to-use prompts for the documents you already write — requirements, acceptance criteria, process maps, business cases, test scripts. Copy one, paste it into Copilot or ChatGPT, and edit the draft it gives you back.

New to using AI at work?

You do not need to know anything about AI to use this. The Start here page covers the whole thing in about five minutes: what you need, how to use a prompt, and what to check before you trust what comes back.

What you get that a blank chat box does not

It asks before it writes
Every prompt makes the assistant ask you up to five questions first. That is the difference between a generic answer and one about your project.
It flags what it made up
AI assistants invent figures, dates and standards rather than admit they do not know. These prompts forbid that, and make the assistant tag anything unverified as [TBC] and anything it suggested itself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Those tags become your checklist.
It tells you where the answer came from
Each prompt ends by asking the assistant to explain how it reached its answer and list every assumption it made, so you can check it rather than trust it.
It reminds you about sensitive data
Each prompt tells the assistant to stop and warn you if what you pasted looks like real personal information.
The output has a shape you can check
Prompts specify the actual structure — named sections, table columns, how many test cases of each kind — so you can hold the draft up against what you asked for.

Where these came from

They were written by a working BA practice for its own use, then made organisation-neutral so anyone can adopt them: no client names, no sector assumptions, no country-specific rules. Every prompt was reviewed against a published quality standard before it was published here.

Two edits make them yours: add your organisation and sector to the context section, and swap each worked example for one from your own domain. The example is the part that most changes the quality of what you get back.

Help make them better

If you write a prompt that works, or find that one of these does not, both are worth more than anything else here. Contributors keep their name on their prompts. How to contribute.
