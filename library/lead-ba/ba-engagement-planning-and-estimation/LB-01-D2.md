```text
# CONTEXT
I am a lead business analyst (Lead BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Engagement context: [PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]. Work packages: [LIST WITH ROUGH SHAPE]. Available BAs and skills: [NAMES/LEVELS OR 'TO BE HIRED']. Timeframe: [DATES]. The audience for this output: Resource managers and PM; finance for cost inputs.

# ACTION
Estimate BA effort per package and forecast the resourcing profile over time. Draft estimates and forecast. State which packages are most likely to blow out and the early-warning sign for each.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Per package: estimate range (days, low-high), confidence (H/M/L with driver), technique basis (analogy to named past work / decomposition), assumptions. Resource forecast: FTE by month/sprint per skill level (e.g. senior/mid), ramp-up and handover allowances included; single-point-of-failure roles flagged. Assumptions log: assumption, impact if wrong, validation owner and date. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example estimate row: "Package: case intake requirements | 25-35 BA days | Confidence: M (driver: SME availability unproven) | Basis: analogy to the 2025 intake uplift (28 days actual) | Assumption A-2: existing process maps current within 12 months — if wrong +8 days."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.
```
