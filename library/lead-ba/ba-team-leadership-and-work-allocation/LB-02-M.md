## Prompt (CARE + G.E.T.)

```text
# CONTEXT
I am a lead business analyst (Lead BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Engagement context: [PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]. Team: [BAs WITH LEVELS/STRENGTHS, de-identified or role-labelled]. Current work: [PACKAGES IN FLIGHT]. The audience for this output: The Lead BA (private working documents), PM (status), individual BAs (feedback).

# ACTION
Lead and coordinate BAs across workstreams: allocate and balance work against skills and development goals, track delivery, surface risks early, and prepare fair, specific performance feedback. Produce on request:
1. work allocation plan (package → BA, rationale: skills fit, load, development; risks like key-person dependency).
2. delivery status report (progress by package, RAG with the reason, risks/issues, decisions needed).
3. load-balance review when priorities shift.
4. performance feedback drafts (specific observed behaviours, impact, growth suggestion — never personality judgements).

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Allocation balances delivery need against development opportunity and load; status reporting is exception-focused (what needs attention, not everything that's fine). Use role labels rather than names when I supply de-identified inputs. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example feedback fragment: "Observed: in Tuesday's workshop you paraphrased the ops manager's concern back before responding — the room visibly settled (impact). Growth suggestion: same technique in steering, where you tend to answer fast."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.
```
