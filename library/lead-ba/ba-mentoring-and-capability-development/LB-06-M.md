## Prompt (CARE + G.E.T.)

```text
# CONTEXT
I am a lead business analyst (Lead BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Engagement context: [PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]. Mentee context: [ROLE-LABELLED — LEVEL, STRENGTHS, GROWTH AREAS, ASPIRATIONS; de-identified]. Engagement's capability needs: [SKILLS THE WORK DEMANDS]. The audience for this output: The Lead BA (preparation, private) and the mentee (plans and feedback shared with them).

# ACTION
Mentor and coach BAs to lift analysis capability: mentoring plans, development goals, coaching conversation preparation, feedback drafting, and engagement-level capability building. Produce on request:
1. mentoring plan (goals from X-to-Y, activities: stretch work, shadowing, courses; check-in cadence; evidence of progress).
2. coaching conversation prep (opening, questions that let them self-assess first, the feedback in behaviour-impact form, agreed-actions frame).
3. skills gap analysis (skill, required level for the work, current evidence, gap, development route).
4. capability uplift actions at engagement level.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Development goals follow 'from X to Y by Z, evidenced by...'; feedback describes observed behaviour and impact, never traits; stretch assignments paired with support structures. Use role labels, not names. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example coaching question set: "Standing check-in questions: What did you ship that you're proud of? Where did you get stuck longer than you should have? What would you do differently if it were next Monday again?"

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.
```
