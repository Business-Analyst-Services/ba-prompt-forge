## Prompt (CARE + G.E.T.)

```text
# CONTEXT
I am an agile business analyst (Agile BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Squad context: [SQUAD NAME, PRODUCT/SYSTEM, CADENCE e.g. 2-week sprints, TOOL e.g. Jira]. Definition of Ready / Done: [PASTE OR 'STANDARD']. Uplift focus: [DoR/DoD / ANALYSIS PRACTICES / CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT]. Current pain points: [WHAT'S NOT WORKING]. Current artefacts: [PASTE EXISTING DoR/DoD/WORKING AGREEMENTS IF ANY]. The audience for this output: The squad (owners of their ways of working); Lead BA/practice interested in patterns.

# ACTION
Embed and improve analysis practices within the squad: uplift Definitions of Ready and Done, improve how analysis supports flow, and run continuous improvement that actually sticks. Produce on request:
1. DoR/DoD drafts tailored to the squad's context and pain points (each criterion: what it means, why it's there, how checked).
2. analysis ways-of-working guidelines (how analysis feeds the flow: elaboration cadence, BA involvement per ceremony, artefact expectations).
3. practice gap analysis from my description (gap, consequence seen, candidate practice, effort).
4. improvement backlog with experiments (hypothesis, trial period, success signal).

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Practices must be team-owned: draft proposals as starting points for the squad to adapt, not mandates. Every practice change paired with 'how we'll know it helped'. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example practice gap: "Gap: no elaboration cadence — stories elaborated in the sprint they're built. Consequence seen: 3 stories bounced, spike run mid-sprint. Candidate: rolling 1.5-sprint elaboration horizon with weekly 1-hr session. Effort: low."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.
```
