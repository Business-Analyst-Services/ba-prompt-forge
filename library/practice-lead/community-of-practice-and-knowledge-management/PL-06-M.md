## Prompt (CARE + G.E.T.)

```text
# CONTEXT
I am a BA practice lead. I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Practice context: [PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]. Community state: [ACTIVE / SPUTTERING / NONE]. Knowledge state: [WHERE ARTEFACTS AND LESSONS LIVE TODAY, e.g. Confluence spaces]. Pain: [WHAT KNOWLEDGE KEEPS GETTING LOST OR RE-CREATED]. The audience for this output: Practice BAs (the community), Practice Lead (curation).

# ACTION
Foster the BA community, knowledge sharing and reuse of practice assets: community sessions people attend by choice, a knowledge base people actually search first, and lessons that change behaviour rather than filling a register. Produce on request:
1. CoP session plans (topic, format — case clinic, artefact teardown, lightning talks; prep; the takeaway artefact).
2. topic backlog from my pain points, prioritised by reuse value.
3. knowledge base structure (organised by delivery context and L3 service, with contribution guidelines and quality bar for reusable assets).
4. lessons-learned entries rewritten from my raw material into next-time guidance, tagged for retrieval.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Community sessions have a format and a takeaway, not just a speaker; knowledge base designed around retrieval moments ('I'm starting a procurement — what exists?'); lessons written as reusable guidance ('next time, do X') not incident reports. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example lesson rewrite: "Raw: 'vendor demo scripts were late again.' Rewritten rule: 'For any vendor demo, issue scenario scripts with data setup 10 business days ahead and hold a dry-run 3 days prior — late scripts in the CRM procurement cost a full evaluation round.' Tags: procurement, demo, evaluation."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.
```
