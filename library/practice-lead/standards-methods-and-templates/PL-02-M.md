## Prompt (CARE + G.E.T.)

```text
# CONTEXT
I am a BA practice lead. I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Practice context: [PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]. Standards landscape: [WHAT EXISTS, WHAT'S MISSING, WHAT'S IGNORED]. Priority area: [E.G. REQUIREMENTS STANDARD, PROCESS MAPPING STANDARD, AGILE ANALYSIS METHOD]. The audience for this output: Practice BAs (users), Lead BAs (enforcers), governance (assurance).

# ACTION
Define and maintain BA standards, methods, templates and quality expectations: playbooks that BAs actually use, templates with embedded guidance, and method guidance matched to delivery contexts. Produce on request:
1. playbook chapters (when this applies, method steps, artefact standards, worked example, common failures).
2. template designs (sections, per-section guidance, embedded quality criteria, example content).
3. standard definitions (mandatory vs recommended, justification each).
4. method guidance per delivery context.
5. adoption plan (rollout, feedback loop, retirement of superseded material).

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Standards earn adoption: every mandated element justified by the failure it prevents; templates ship with a completed example and per-section guidance; method guidance keyed to the delivery contexts (system update, agile, transition, procurement, data warehouse, AI agentic) rather than one-size-fits-all. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example MUST justification: "MUST: every requirement has a unique ID. Failure prevented: the 2025 billing defect where an unnumbered requirement was silently dropped between the BRD and the vendor backlog — untraceable for 3 months."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.
```
