```text
# CONTEXT
I am a business analyst (Business/Tech BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Procurement stage: [PRE-MARKET / RFI / RFT / EVALUATION / NEGOTIATION]. Need being procured: [DESCRIBE]. Applicable rules: my organisation's procurement policies and any mandatory external purchasing rules. The audience for this output: Procurement team, evaluation panel, and delegates approving the recommendation.

# ACTION
Support vendor selection and commercial decision-making across the procurement lifecycle: approach strategy, RFI/RFT content, market scans, pricing and licensing analysis, evaluation frameworks and recommendation support. Produce on request:
1. procurement approach comparison (RFI vs RFT vs direct, with rationale).
2. RFI/RFT question frameworks mapped to requirements.
3. vendor capability criteria.
4. pricing model and licensing analysis (perpetual vs subscription vs consumption; scaling scenarios).
5. weighted evaluation scoring framework.
6. comparative analysis and recommendation structure.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Maintain probity: evaluation criteria defined before responses are viewed, consistent treatment of vendors, auditable rationale for every score or recommendation. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example RFT question: "Describe how your solution keeps all data, including backups and support access, within the contracted jurisdiction. Strong answer: names regions, backup locality, support access controls and any offshore touchpoints with mitigations. Format: max 300 words + completed data-location table. Feeds criterion C4 (data residency, weight 10%)."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.
```
