## Prompt (CARE + G.E.T.)

```text
# CONTEXT
I am a technical business analyst (Technical BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Solution: [DESCRIBE, INCLUDING DATA TOUCHED AND USER POPULATIONS]. Obligations: applicable privacy legislation, any additional legislation covering sensitive information, information security standards, and my organisation's security policies; [OTHERS]. Risk profile: [KNOWN CONCERNS]. The audience for this output: Security architects, privacy officer, compliance and audit; delivery team implementing controls.

# ACTION
Ensure the solution meets security, privacy and regulatory obligations: security requirements, access management and RBAC, identity and trust, data sovereignty, privacy assessment inputs and compliance controls with audit evidence. Produce on request:
1. information classification assessment (data element, classification, handling requirements).
2. security requirements (authentication incl. SSO/MFA needs, authorisation, encryption at rest/in transit, logging/audit, vulnerability management).
3. RBAC matrix (role vs function vs permission with least-privilege and segregation-of-duties checks).
4. privacy assessment inputs (data flows, purposes, consent basis, retention, disclosure, cross-border touchpoints) structured for a PIA.
5. compliance controls mapping (obligation → control → evidence) for audit.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured technical-BA writing: requirements numbered and testable, expressed as needs and constraints rather than design decisions (pre-solution) or as precise specifications (post-solution). Tables over prose where possible; expand acronyms on first use. Frame requirements from obligations ('to satisfy information-security standard element X, the solution shall...'). Never present analysis as legal advice — outputs support, not replace, security and privacy specialist review. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example requirement: "SEC-04: All user access shall be via corporate SSO with MFA; no local accounts. SEC-11: Personal and sensitive information shall be encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.2+). RBAC example row: Role: Case Officer | View case: assigned team only | Approve payment: no (segregation: approval sits with the Team Leader)."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.
```
