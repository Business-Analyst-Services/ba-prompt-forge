# Master – Stakeholder Analysis & Engagement

Derived from `BT-08-M` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Business/Tech BA / Stakeholder Analysis & Engagement, scored 19/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing stakeholder analysis & engagement work and needs one of:
- Stakeholder Register
- Stakeholder/SME Engagement Plan
- Workshops
- Showcases

The audience for whatever you produce is: **BA and project leadership using the plan; some outputs (comms) go direct to stakeholders**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[LIST KNOWN STAKEHOLDERS/AREAS, INTERNAL AND EXTERNAL — e.g. operations teams, field staff, delivery partners, employee representatives, customer groups, IT, vendors]`
3. `[ANY KNOWN POLITICS OR HISTORY]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- stakeholder register (name/role, area, interest, influence, attitude, needs, engagement approach, cadence, owner)
- interest/influence matrix with tailored strategies, alliances and risk flags
- RACI for pasted activities with bottleneck checks (3+ Consulted, missing Accountable)
- conflict resolution strategy for two named positions (underlying needs, 3 approaches, recommended talking points)
- audience-tailored update messages. Ask me about organisational dynamics before mapping

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Engagement strategies must be specific to the stakeholder ('fortnightly 1:1 with the operations manager focused on X'), never generic ('keep informed').

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example register row: "Stakeholder: Operations Manager (N. Patel) | Interest: fears productivity dip during cutover | Influence: High (owns rostering) | Attitude: neutral | Approach: fortnightly 1:1 focused on transition workload modelling; involve in UAT scheduling decisions | Cadence: fortnightly | Owner: Senior BA."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
