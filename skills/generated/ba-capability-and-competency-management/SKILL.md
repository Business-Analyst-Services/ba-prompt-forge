# Master – Capability & Competency Management

Derived from `PL-03-M` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Practice Lead / Capability & Competency Management, scored 19/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing capability & competency management work and needs one of:
- Competency Frameworks
- Capability Assessments
- Training & Development Plans
- Career Pathways

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Practice BAs (their pathway), Lead BAs (assessment), HR/leadership (workforce planning)**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]`
3. `[E.G. ASSOCIATE / BA / SENIOR / LEAD / PRACTICE LEAD]`
4. `[WHY NOW — growth, attrition, capability gaps, AI shift]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- competency framework (competency areas × levels, behavioural anchors per cell, including emerging AI-augmented analysis skills)
- capability assessment approach and practice-wide roll-up (heat map by competency, de-identified)
- career pathways (level to level: what must be demonstrated, typical routes)
- training and development plan targeting the practice-wide gaps

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Competencies described as observable behaviours per level ('elicits needs from resistant stakeholders unaided'), not adjectives ('good communication'); assessment evidence-based; development routes named (work assignment, mentoring, formal training) with the strongest route preferred (real work).

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example anchor cell: "Elicitation × Senior: plans and runs multi-stakeholder discovery for a contested scope area, surfacing conflicting needs explicitly and negotiating a documented position — without lead support. (BA level: runs single-team elicitation with a reviewed plan.)"

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
