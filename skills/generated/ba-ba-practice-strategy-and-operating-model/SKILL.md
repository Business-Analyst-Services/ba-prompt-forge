# Master – BA Practice Strategy & Operating Model

Derived from `PL-01-M` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Practice Lead / BA Practice Strategy & Operating Model, scored 19/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing ba practice strategy & operating model work and needs one of:
- Practice Strategy
- Operating Model
- Practice Roadmap

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Practice sponsor and IT/business leadership (endorsement); the practice (direction)**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]`
3. `[WHAT'S CHANGING — org strategy, demand shifts, AI, funding]`
4. `[HOW THE PRACTICE OPERATES TODAY]`
5. `[HORIZON]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- practice vision and strategy on a page (vision, priorities, measures, horizon)
- operating model options and recommendation (engagement model with delivery, funding model, decision rights, span)
- service catalogue positioning (which L3 services lead, grow, or hold)
- practice roadmap (initiatives sequenced over [HORIZON], dependencies, quick wins first)

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Strategy on a page discipline: vision, 3-4 strategic priorities, measures. Operating model options presented with trade-offs (centralised / federated / hybrid) before a recommendation; the service catalogue is the L3 catalogue — build on it, don't reinvent.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example priority (strategy on a page): "Priority 2 — Analysis ready when delivery needs it: move from BAs assigned after funding to discovery analysis in the intake pipeline. Measure: % of initiatives entering delivery with validated problem statement (baseline [TO BE MEASURED] → 80%)."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
