# Master – Senior Stakeholder & Sponsor Engagement

Derived from `LB-05-M` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Lead BA / Senior Stakeholder & Sponsor Engagement, scored 19/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing senior stakeholder & sponsor engagement work and needs one of:
- Steering Committee Inputs
- Escalation Papers
- Decision Records
- Engagement Summaries

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Sponsors, steering committees, executive stakeholders**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]`
3. `[ROLES — e.g. sponsor, EGM, CIO — and what each cares about]`
4. `[LIVE DECISIONS, ESCALATIONS, SENSITIVITIES]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- escalation paper (issue, impact quantified where possible, options with consequences, the specific ask, needed-by date)
- steering committee input (status headline, decisions sought with recommendations, risks worth their attention)
- decision record (decision, decider, rationale, alternatives rejected, impacts)
- engagement summary after key meetings (what was agreed, positions shifted, follow-ups)

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Executive economy: the ask in the first two sentences; one page unless the decision justifies more; options always include consequences of deciding nothing.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example decisions-sought block: "Decision 2: approve descope of bulk re-queue to R3. Recommendation: approve. Consequence of deferral: R2 date moves 2 wks OR testing compressed below agreed risk floor. Cost of descope: ops manual workaround ~20 min/day for 6 wks."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
