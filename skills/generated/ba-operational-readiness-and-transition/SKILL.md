# Master – Operational Readiness & Transition

Derived from `BT-04-M` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Business/Tech BA / Operational Readiness & Transition, scored 19/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing operational readiness & transition work and needs one of:
- Showcases
- Dev walkthroughs
- Desk checks
- System Transition Plan
- Operational Readiness support

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Business readiness leads, support teams, change managers and the go-live decision forum**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[DESCRIBE]`
3. `[DATE]`
4. `[BUSINESS TEAMS, SUPPORT TEAMS, AGENTS]`
5. `[WHAT CHANGES FOR WHOM]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- transition requirements (process cutover, data cutover dependencies, access, training, support)
- showcase/walkthrough session designs with scenarios to demonstrate
- desk check scripts for business validation
- system transition plan structure (phases, entry/exit criteria, rollback triggers)
- operational readiness checklist with owner and evidence per item

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Readiness expressed as verifiable criteria ('training completed by X roles', 'support articles published'), not activities.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example readiness criterion: "Item: operations team training | Criterion: ≥95% of case officers have completed module CT-3 and passed the scenario assessment | Owner: Business Readiness Lead | Evidence: learning system completion report | Due: go-live minus 10 business days."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
