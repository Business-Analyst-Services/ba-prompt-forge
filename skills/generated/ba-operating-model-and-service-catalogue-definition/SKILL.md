# Operating Model & Service Catalogue Definition

Derived from `PL-01-D2` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Practice Lead / BA Practice Strategy & Operating Model, scored 20/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing ba practice strategy & operating model work and needs one of:
- Operating Model
- Service Catalogue
- Practice Roadmap

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Practice sponsor and PMO (agreement); delivery leaders (the customer view)**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]`
3. `[HOW BAs GET ASSIGNED TODAY]`
4. `[WHAT'S NOT WORKING]`
5. `[REFERENCE — 42 services across Business/Business-Tech/Technical/Agile/Lead/Practice roles]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- Design how the practice engages with delivery and formalise the service catalogue around the L3 services. Draft model and catalogue. State the failure mode of the recommended model and the guardrail against it.

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Operating model: options (centralised pool / embedded-federated / hybrid) assessed against the user's friction points; recommendation with decision rights (who assigns, who sets standards, who owns quality), funding/chargeback note, and interfaces to PMO and delivery. Service catalogue: the L3 services with, per service: when to request it, what you get (deliverables), how to engage. Transition roadmap from current to target.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example decision-rights line: "Assignment: PMO requests via the service catalogue; Practice Lead assigns (delivery may request, not select, individuals). Standards: Practice Lead owns; delivery variances need written practice sign-off. Quality: Lead BA on each program owns artefact QA against practice checklists."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
