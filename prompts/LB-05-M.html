Master – Senior Stakeholder & Sponsor Engagement

Manage alignment, escalations and decision-making with senior stakeholders and sponsors: prepare escalation papers, steering inputs, decision records and engagement summaries that respect executive attention. Produce on request

Writes: Steering Committee Inputs, Escalation Papers, Decision Records, Engagement Summaries For: Sponsors, steering committees, executive stakeholders

Sets the assistant up to work with you across this whole area, offering several things it can produce. If you only need one document, use a deliverable prompt instead. Filed under Senior Stakeholder & Sponsor Engagement for a Lead BA. Reference LB-05-M. Quality score 19/20.

1. Have these ready

You will replace each of these in the prompt below:

  • [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
  • [PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]
  • [ROLES — e.g. sponsor, EGM, CIO — and what each cares about]
  • [LIVE DECISIONS, ESCALATIONS, SENSITIVITIES]

Do not know one? Leave it as it is and say so — the assistant will ask.

Before you paste anything in

My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding.

In short: take out real names, case numbers and anything else identifying, or make up an example instead. See de-identified data.

2. Copy the prompt

# CONTEXT
I am a lead business analyst (Lead BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Engagement context: [PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]. Senior stakeholders: [ROLES — e.g. sponsor, EGM, CIO — and what each cares about]. Current matters: [LIVE DECISIONS, ESCALATIONS, SENSITIVITIES]. The audience for this output: Sponsors, steering committees, executive stakeholders.

# ACTION
Manage alignment, escalations and decision-making with senior stakeholders and sponsors: prepare escalation papers, steering inputs, decision records and engagement summaries that respect executive attention. Produce on request:
1. escalation paper (issue, impact quantified where possible, options with consequences, the specific ask, needed-by date).
2. steering committee input (status headline, decisions sought with recommendations, risks worth their attention).
3. decision record (decision, decider, rationale, alternatives rejected, impacts).
4. engagement summary after key meetings (what was agreed, positions shifted, follow-ups).

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Executive economy: the ask in the first two sentences; one page unless the decision justifies more; options always include consequences of deciding nothing. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example decisions-sought block: "Decision 2: approve descope of bulk re-queue to R3. Recommendation: approve. Consequence of deferral: R2 date moves 2 wks OR testing compressed below agreed risk floor. Cost of descope: ops manual workaround ~20 min/day for 6 wks."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.

3. Paste it into your AI assistant

Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — any of them. Here is what should happen:

  1. It asks you questions first (up to 5). Answer them — this is what makes the draft about your project rather than a generic one.
  2. It writes the document in the structure the prompt asked for.
  3. It explains how it got there and lists the assumptions it made, so you can check its reasoning and not just its output.

Anything tagged [TBC] it could not verify. Anything tagged [PROPOSED — VALIDATE] it suggested itself. Those tags are your to-do list. Treat the whole thing as a first draft and read every line before it goes to anyone.

# Master – Senior Stakeholder & Sponsor Engagement

Derived from `LB-05-M` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Lead BA / Senior Stakeholder & Sponsor Engagement, scored 19/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing senior stakeholder & sponsor engagement work and needs one of:
- Steering Committee Inputs
- Escalation Papers
- Decision Records
- Engagement Summaries

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Sponsors, steering committees, executive stakeholders**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]`
3. `[ROLES — e.g. sponsor, EGM, CIO — and what each cares about]`
4. `[LIVE DECISIONS, ESCALATIONS, SENSITIVITIES]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- escalation paper (issue, impact quantified where possible, options with consequences, the specific ask, needed-by date)
- steering committee input (status headline, decisions sought with recommendations, risks worth their attention)
- decision record (decision, decider, rationale, alternatives rejected, impacts)
- engagement summary after key meetings (what was agreed, positions shifted, follow-ups)

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Executive economy: the ask in the first two sentences; one page unless the decision justifies more; options always include consequences of deciding nothing.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example decisions-sought block: "Decision 2: approve descope of bulk re-queue to R3. Recommendation: approve. Consequence of deferral: R2 date moves 2 wks OR testing compressed below agreed risk floor. Cost of descope: ops manual workaround ~20 min/day for 6 wks."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
