You are: a BA practice lead.

Target deliverables: Recruitment Packs, Onboarding Guides

What you need to fill in

  • [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
  • [PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]
  • [LEVEL AND FOCUS — e.g. Senior Technical BA]
  • [PASTE RELEVANT COLUMN OR 'STANDARD']
  • [CONTEXT]

CARE + G.E.T.

The library's native format. Context, Action, Result/Format, Example, plus Generate -> Explain -> Test. Lossless: every rubric criterion has somewhere to go.

# CONTEXT
I am a BA practice lead. I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Practice context: [PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]. Role to define: [LEVEL AND FOCUS — e.g. Senior Technical BA]. Competency framework: [PASTE RELEVANT COLUMN OR 'STANDARD']. Team it joins: [CONTEXT]. The audience for this output: Candidates and recruiters (role description); the new starter and their buddy (onboarding).

# ACTION
Draft the role description and the onboarding pack that gets the hire productive fast. Draft both. Cut any 'requirement' that's really a wish — inflated musts shrink the candidate pool for nothing.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Role description: purpose (why this role exists in the organisation); accountabilities (outcomes, not task lists); competencies required (from the framework, behavioural); nice-to-haves separated honestly from musts; what success looks like at 3/6/12 months. Onboarding: week-by-week first month (access, systems, standards, key people); the 5 practice standards to read first; buddy model; 30/60/90-day check-in questions. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example 3-month success line: "At 3 months: independently produces reviewed-quality requirements for a medium change; knows the core business domains well enough to challenge an SME's first answer; has run one workshop with support."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.

# Role Description & Onboarding Pack

Derived from `PL-04-D2` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Practice Lead / Workforce & Resource Management, scored 20/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing workforce & resource management work and needs one of:
- Recruitment Packs
- Onboarding Guides

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Candidates and recruiters (role description); the new starter and their buddy (onboarding)**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]`
3. `[LEVEL AND FOCUS — e.g. Senior Technical BA]`
4. `[PASTE RELEVANT COLUMN OR 'STANDARD']`
5. `[CONTEXT]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- Draft the role description and the onboarding pack that gets the hire productive fast. Draft both. Cut any 'requirement' that's really a wish — inflated musts shrink the candidate pool for nothing.

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Role description: purpose (why this role exists in the organisation); accountabilities (outcomes, not task lists); competencies required (from the framework, behavioural); nice-to-haves separated honestly from musts; what success looks like at 3/6/12 months. Onboarding: week-by-week first month (access, systems, standards, key people); the 5 practice standards to read first; buddy model; 30/60/90-day check-in questions.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example 3-month success line: "At 3 months: independently produces reviewed-quality requirements for a medium change; knows the core business domains well enough to challenge an SME's first answer; has run one workshop with support."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
