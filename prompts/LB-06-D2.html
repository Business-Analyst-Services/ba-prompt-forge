Who it is for

You are: a lead business analyst (Lead BA).
The audience for the output: Lead BA and resource/practice managers.

Target deliverables: Skills gap analysis, Capability Uplift Actions

What you need to fill in

  • [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
  • [PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]
  • [LIST]
  • [ROLE-LABELLED: LEVELS, RECENT WORK, KNOWN STRENGTHS/GAPS]

Before you paste anything in: My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding.

CARE + G.E.T.

The library's native format. Context, Action, Result/Format, Example, plus Generate -> Explain -> Test. Lossless: every rubric criterion has somewhere to go.

# CONTEXT
I am a lead business analyst (Lead BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Engagement context: [PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]. Work ahead and skills it demands: [LIST]. Team skills evidence: [ROLE-LABELLED: LEVELS, RECENT WORK, KNOWN STRENGTHS/GAPS]. The audience for this output: Lead BA and resource/practice managers.

# ACTION
Map the team's skills against what the engagement needs, and plan the uplift that closes delivery-critical gaps. Draft matrix and uplift plan. Single-person coverage of a critical skill is a risk even with no gap — flag those too.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Matrix: skill; level required (working/proficient/expert with anchors); per role-labelled BA: current level with evidence; team coverage verdict. Gap analysis: delivery-critical gaps (no coverage or single-person coverage) vs development-nice-to-haves. Uplift actions per critical gap: route (pairing/training/hiring/contract), time-to-competent, interim risk mitigation. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example coverage verdict: "Skill: interface specification | Required: proficient ×2 (integration-heavy phase) | Current: expert ×1 (Senior BA-2), none else above working | Verdict: single-person risk. Uplift: BA-4 pairing (time-to-proficient ~8 wks); interim: BA-2 leave embargo during interface sprints — flag to PM."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.

# Skills Gap Analysis (Team)

Derived from `LB-06-D2` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Lead BA / BA Mentoring & Capability Development, scored 20/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing ba mentoring & capability development work and needs one of:
- Skills gap analysis
- Capability Uplift Actions

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Lead BA and resource/practice managers**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[PROGRAM/PROJECT, NUMBER OF BAs AND WORKSTREAMS, DELIVERY METHOD]`
3. `[LIST]`
4. `[ROLE-LABELLED: LEVELS, RECENT WORK, KNOWN STRENGTHS/GAPS]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- Map the team's skills against what the engagement needs, and plan the uplift that closes delivery-critical gaps. Draft matrix and uplift plan. Single-person coverage of a critical skill is a risk even with no gap — flag those too.

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Matrix: skill; level required (working/proficient/expert with anchors); per role-labelled BA: current level with evidence; team coverage verdict. Gap analysis: delivery-critical gaps (no coverage or single-person coverage) vs development-nice-to-haves. Uplift actions per critical gap: route (pairing/training/hiring/contract), time-to-competent, interim risk mitigation.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example coverage verdict: "Skill: interface specification | Required: proficient ×2 (integration-heavy phase) | Current: expert ×1 (Senior BA-2), none else above working | Verdict: single-person risk. Uplift: BA-4 pairing (time-to-proficient ~8 wks); interim: BA-2 leave embargo during interface sprints — flag to PM."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
