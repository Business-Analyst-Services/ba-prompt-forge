Master – Just-in-Time Requirements Elaboration

Progressively elaborate requirements on cadence so stories are ready ahead of each sprint: manage Definition of Ready compliance, elaborate just-in-time (right detail now, defer the rest), and run spikes for uncertainty. Produce on request

Writes: Definition of Ready Compliance, Elaborated User Stories, Acceptance Criteria, Example/BDD Scenarios For: Squad (build and test from the stories), product owner (readiness decisions)

Sets the assistant up to work with you across this whole area, offering several things it can produce. If you only need one document, use a deliverable prompt instead. Filed under Just-in-Time Requirements Elaboration for a Agile BA. Reference AB-03-M. Quality score 19/20.

1. Have these ready

You will replace each of these in the prompt below:

  • [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
  • [SQUAD NAME, PRODUCT/SYSTEM, CADENCE e.g. 2-week sprints, TOOL e.g. Jira]
  • [PASTE OR 'STANDARD']
  • [E.G. STORIES FOR NEXT 1-2 SPRINTS]
  • [PASTE OR REFERENCE]

Do not know one? Leave it as it is and say so — the assistant will ask.

Before you paste anything in

My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding.

In short: take out real names, case numbers and anything else identifying, or make up an example instead. See de-identified data.

2. Copy the prompt

# CONTEXT
I am an agile business analyst (Agile BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Squad context: [SQUAD NAME, PRODUCT/SYSTEM, CADENCE e.g. 2-week sprints, TOOL e.g. Jira]. Definition of Ready / Done: [PASTE OR 'STANDARD']. Elaboration horizon: [E.G. STORIES FOR NEXT 1-2 SPRINTS]. Upcoming backlog: [PASTE OR REFERENCE]. The audience for this output: Squad (build and test from the stories), product owner (readiness decisions).

# ACTION
Progressively elaborate requirements on cadence so stories are ready ahead of each sprint: manage Definition of Ready compliance, elaborate just-in-time (right detail now, defer the rest), and run spikes for uncertainty. Produce on request:
1. readiness assessment of pasted stories against the DoR (met/not met per criterion, blocking gaps, who can close each).
2. story elaboration (description, business rules, acceptance criteria, BDD example scenarios with data).
3. 'now vs later' analysis — what detail is needed this sprint vs deferrable.
4. spike definitions (question to answer, timebox, done-means, decision the outcome feeds).

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Elaborate only what the next sprint needs — flag over-elaboration as waste. BDD scenarios in Given/When/Then with concrete example data, covering happy, negative and boundary cases. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example now-vs-later call: "Now (blocks sprint): which document types trigger the flag. Later (defer to r2 elaboration): bulk-clear behaviour — not in this sprint's stories, elaborating now is waste."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.

3. Paste it into your AI assistant

Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — any of them. Here is what should happen:

  1. It asks you questions first (up to 5). Answer them — this is what makes the draft about your project rather than a generic one.
  2. It writes the document in the structure the prompt asked for.
  3. It explains how it got there and lists the assumptions it made, so you can check its reasoning and not just its output.

Anything tagged [TBC] it could not verify. Anything tagged [PROPOSED — VALIDATE] it suggested itself. Those tags are your to-do list. Treat the whole thing as a first draft and read every line before it goes to anyone.

# Master – Just-in-Time Requirements Elaboration

Derived from `AB-03-M` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Agile BA / Just-in-Time Requirements Elaboration, scored 19/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing just-in-time requirements elaboration work and needs one of:
- Definition of Ready Compliance
- Elaborated User Stories
- Acceptance Criteria
- Example/BDD Scenarios

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Squad (build and test from the stories), product owner (readiness decisions)**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[SQUAD NAME, PRODUCT/SYSTEM, CADENCE e.g. 2-week sprints, TOOL e.g. Jira]`
3. `[PASTE OR 'STANDARD']`
4. `[E.G. STORIES FOR NEXT 1-2 SPRINTS]`
5. `[PASTE OR REFERENCE]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- readiness assessment of pasted stories against the DoR (met/not met per criterion, blocking gaps, who can close each)
- story elaboration (description, business rules, acceptance criteria, BDD example scenarios with data)
- 'now vs later' analysis — what detail is needed this sprint vs deferrable
- spike definitions (question to answer, timebox, done-means, decision the outcome feeds)

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Elaborate only what the next sprint needs — flag over-elaboration as waste. BDD scenarios in Given/When/Then with concrete example data, covering happy, negative and boundary cases.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
