Master – Discovery & Problem Definition

Help me define the real problem and the case for change: separate symptoms from root causes, quantify impact, frame the opportunity, test strategic alignment, and draft business case inputs and initial risk assessments. Produce on request

Writes: Problem Statements, Opportunity Assessments, Business Needs Statements, Business Case Inputs, Strategic Alignment Assessments, Risk Assessment Support For: Sponsor, business case authors and investment governance forums

Sets the assistant up to work with you across this whole area, offering several things it can produce. If you only need one document, use a deliverable prompt instead. Filed under Business Project Discovery & Problem Definition for a Business BA. Reference BB-03-M. Quality score 19/20.

1. Have these ready

You will replace each of these in the prompt below:

  • [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
  • [WHAT PROMPTED IT — incident, audit, strategy, pain point]
  • [LIST]
  • [PASTE, OR 'NONE SUPPLIED']
  • [TO BE QUANTIFIED]

Do not know one? Leave it as it is and say so — the assistant will ask.

Before you paste anything in

My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding.

In short: take out real names, case numbers and anything else identifying, or make up an example instead. See de-identified data.

2. Copy the prompt

# CONTEXT
I am a business analyst (Business BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Trigger for this work: [WHAT PROMPTED IT — incident, audit, strategy, pain point]. Known symptoms: [LIST]. Relevant organisational strategic objectives: [PASTE, OR 'NONE SUPPLIED']. The audience for this output: Sponsor, business case authors and investment governance forums.

# ACTION
Help me define the real problem and the case for change: separate symptoms from root causes, quantify impact, frame the opportunity, test strategic alignment, and draft business case inputs and initial risk assessments. Produce on request:
1. a problem statement (situation, impact, magnitude, consequence of inaction).
2. an opportunity assessment with options at a high level.
3. strategic alignment assessment against the organisation's objectives.
4. an initial risk list (risk, cause, consequence, likelihood, impact, mitigation).

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Use the 5 Whys or fishbone technique for causes; every impact claim needs a number or an explicit '[TO BE QUANTIFIED]' flag. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example problem statement shape: "Customers submitting service requests by phone experience double handling: 40% of requests [TO BE QUANTIFIED] are re-keyed, adding ~3 days delay and creating transcription errors that flow into triage. If unaddressed, timeframe breaches will continue at roughly [X]/quarter."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.

3. Paste it into your AI assistant

Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — any of them. Here is what should happen:

  1. It asks you questions first (up to 5). Answer them — this is what makes the draft about your project rather than a generic one.
  2. It writes the document in the structure the prompt asked for.
  3. It explains how it got there and lists the assumptions it made, so you can check its reasoning and not just its output.

Anything tagged [TBC] it could not verify. Anything tagged [PROPOSED — VALIDATE] it suggested itself. Those tags are your to-do list. Treat the whole thing as a first draft and read every line before it goes to anyone.

# Master – Discovery & Problem Definition

Derived from `BB-03-M` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Business BA / Business Project Discovery & Problem Definition, scored 19/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing business project discovery & problem definition work and needs one of:
- Problem Statements
- Opportunity Assessments
- Business Needs Statements
- Business Case Inputs
- Strategic Alignment Assessments
- Risk Assessment Support

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Sponsor, business case authors and investment governance forums**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[WHAT PROMPTED IT — incident, audit, strategy, pain point]`
3. `[LIST]`
4. `[PASTE, OR 'NONE SUPPLIED']`
5. `[TO BE QUANTIFIED]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- a problem statement (situation, impact, magnitude, consequence of inaction)
- an opportunity assessment with options at a high level
- strategic alignment assessment against the organisation's objectives
- an initial risk list (risk, cause, consequence, likelihood, impact, mitigation)

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Use the 5 Whys or fishbone technique for causes; every impact claim needs a number or an explicit '[TO BE QUANTIFIED]' flag.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example problem statement shape: "Customers submitting service requests by phone experience double handling: 40% of requests [TO BE QUANTIFIED] are re-keyed, adding ~3 days delay and creating transcription errors that flow into triage. If unaddressed, timeframe breaches will continue at roughly [X]/quarter."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
