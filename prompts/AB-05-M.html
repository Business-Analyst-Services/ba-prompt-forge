Master – Agile Metrics & Flow Analysis

Analyse velocity, throughput and flow to inform forecasting and delivery improvement: track velocity and burn, find bottlenecks, and produce forecasts with honest confidence ranges. Produce on request

Writes: Velocity & Burn-down Reports, Cumulative Flow Analysis, Cycle Time Analysis, Delivery Forecasts For: Squad and delivery lead primarily; summarised views for stakeholders

Sets the assistant up to work with you across this whole area, offering several things it can produce. If you only need one document, use a deliverable prompt instead. Filed under Agile Metrics & Flow Analysis for a Agile BA. Reference AB-05-M. Quality score 19/20.

1. Have these ready

You will replace each of these in the prompt below:

  • [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
  • [SQUAD NAME, PRODUCT/SYSTEM, CADENCE e.g. 2-week sprints, TOOL e.g. Jira]
  • [PASTE OR 'STANDARD']
  • [PASTE VELOCITY HISTORY / CYCLE TIMES / CFD DATA / THROUGHPUT — OR DESCRIBE WHAT JIRA CAN EXPORT]
  • [E.G. 'WILL WE HIT THE SEPTEMBER RELEASE?']

Do not know one? Leave it as it is and say so — the assistant will ask.

Before you paste anything in

My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding.

In short: take out real names, case numbers and anything else identifying, or make up an example instead. See de-identified data.

2. Copy the prompt

# CONTEXT
I am an agile business analyst (Agile BA). I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Squad context: [SQUAD NAME, PRODUCT/SYSTEM, CADENCE e.g. 2-week sprints, TOOL e.g. Jira]. Definition of Ready / Done: [PASTE OR 'STANDARD']. Data available: [PASTE VELOCITY HISTORY / CYCLE TIMES / CFD DATA / THROUGHPUT — OR DESCRIBE WHAT JIRA CAN EXPORT]. Question being asked: [E.G. 'WILL WE HIT THE SEPTEMBER RELEASE?']. The audience for this output: Squad and delivery lead primarily; summarised views for stakeholders.

# ACTION
Analyse velocity, throughput and flow to inform forecasting and delivery improvement: track velocity and burn, find bottlenecks, and produce forecasts with honest confidence ranges. Produce on request:
1. velocity/burn analysis (trend, variance, drivers — scope change vs estimation vs interruptions).
2. flow analysis (where work waits, WIP vs limits, cycle time by stage, bottleneck hypothesis with evidence).
3. delivery forecast (range with confidence, method stated, assumptions listed, what would change it).
4. improvement suggestions tied to the specific bottleneck found.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Metrics serve the team, not surveillance: analyse the work and the system, never rank individuals. Forecasts as ranges with stated method (e.g. throughput-based projection), never single dates. Distinguish signal from sprint-to-sprint noise. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example velocity insight: "Velocity fell 34→22 over two sprints, but 9 pts of scope was added mid-sprint each time — the drop is churn, not slower delivery. Countermeasure: sprint-scope change policy, review in retro."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.

3. Paste it into your AI assistant

Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — any of them. Here is what should happen:

  1. It asks you questions first (up to 5). Answer them — this is what makes the draft about your project rather than a generic one.
  2. It writes the document in the structure the prompt asked for.
  3. It explains how it got there and lists the assumptions it made, so you can check its reasoning and not just its output.

Anything tagged [TBC] it could not verify. Anything tagged [PROPOSED — VALIDATE] it suggested itself. Those tags are your to-do list. Treat the whole thing as a first draft and read every line before it goes to anyone.

## When to use this

The user is doing agile metrics & flow analysis work and needs one of:
- Velocity & Burn-down Reports
- Cumulative Flow Analysis
- Cycle Time Analysis
- Delivery Forecasts

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Squad and delivery lead primarily; summarised views for stakeholders**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[SQUAD NAME, PRODUCT/SYSTEM, CADENCE e.g. 2-week sprints, TOOL e.g. Jira]`
3. `[PASTE OR 'STANDARD']`
4. `[PASTE VELOCITY HISTORY / CYCLE TIMES / CFD DATA / THROUGHPUT — OR DESCRIBE WHAT JIRA CAN EXPORT]`
5. `[E.G. 'WILL WE HIT THE SEPTEMBER RELEASE?']`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- velocity/burn analysis (trend, variance, drivers — scope change vs estimation vs interruptions)
- flow analysis (where work waits, WIP vs limits, cycle time by stage, bottleneck hypothesis with evidence)
- delivery forecast (range with confidence, method stated, assumptions listed, what would change it)
- improvement suggestions tied to the specific bottleneck found

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Metrics serve the team, not surveillance: analyse the work and the system, never rank individuals. Forecasts as ranges with stated method (e.g. throughput-based projection), never single dates. Distinguish signal from sprint-to-sprint noise.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.
