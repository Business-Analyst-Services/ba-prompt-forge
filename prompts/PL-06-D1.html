CoP Session Plan & Topic Backlog

Plan the next quarter of CoP sessions people choose to attend, plus the prioritised topic backlog. Draft plan and backlog. Every session must produce a takeaway artefact for the knowledge base — no takeaway, redesign the session.

Writes: Community of Practice Sessions For: Practice BAs; session hosts

Produces one document and stops. Filed under Community of Practice & Knowledge Management for a Practice Lead. Reference PL-06-D1. Quality score 20/20.

1. Have these ready

You will replace each of these in the prompt below:

  • [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
  • [PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]
  • [ATTENDANCE HISTORY, WHAT LANDED WELL]
  • [LIST OR 'MINE FROM RECENT DELIVERY PAIN']

Do not know one? Leave it as it is and say so — the assistant will ask.

Before you paste anything in

My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding.

In short: take out real names, case numbers and anything else identifying, or make up an example instead. See de-identified data.

2. Copy the prompt

# CONTEXT
I am a BA practice lead. I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Practice context: [PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]. Community appetite: [ATTENDANCE HISTORY, WHAT LANDED WELL]. Candidate topics: [LIST OR 'MINE FROM RECENT DELIVERY PAIN']. The audience for this output: Practice BAs; session hosts.

# ACTION
Plan the next quarter of CoP sessions people choose to attend, plus the prioritised topic backlog. Draft plan and backlog. Every session must produce a takeaway artefact for the knowledge base — no takeaway, redesign the session.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Per session: topic; why now (tied to live delivery pain); format (case clinic / artefact teardown / lightning talks / external speaker) matched to the topic; prep asks (small); the takeaway (template, checklist, decision guide attendees leave with); host. Quarter plan: 1 session/month, formats varied. Backlog: topic, reuse value (how many BAs hit this), effort, suggested format. Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example session design: "Session: Interface specs teardown (case clinic). Why now: 3 programs writing interface specs, quality varying. Format: 2 real (de-identified) specs reviewed live against the checklist. Prep: authors volunteer, 10-min read. Takeaway: annotated exemplar spec for the knowledge base. Host: Senior Tech BA."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.

3. Paste it into your AI assistant

Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — any of them. Here is what should happen:

  1. It asks you questions first (up to 5). Answer them — this is what makes the draft about your project rather than a generic one.
  2. It writes the document in the structure the prompt asked for.
  3. It explains how it got there and lists the assumptions it made, so you can check its reasoning and not just its output.

Anything tagged [TBC] it could not verify. Anything tagged [PROPOSED — VALIDATE] it suggested itself. Those tags are your to-do list. Treat the whole thing as a first draft and read every line before it goes to anyone.

# CoP Session Plan & Topic Backlog

Derived from `PL-06-D1` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Practice Lead / Community of Practice & Knowledge Management, scored 20/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing community of practice & knowledge management work and needs one of:
- Community of Practice Sessions

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Practice BAs; session hosts**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]`
3. `[ATTENDANCE HISTORY, WHAT LANDED WELL]`
4. `[LIST OR 'MINE FROM RECENT DELIVERY PAIN']`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- Plan the next quarter of CoP sessions people choose to attend, plus the prioritised topic backlog. Draft plan and backlog. Every session must produce a takeaway artefact for the knowledge base — no takeaway, redesign the session.

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Per session: topic; why now (tied to live delivery pain); format (case clinic / artefact teardown / lightning talks / external speaker) matched to the topic; prep asks (small); the takeaway (template, checklist, decision guide attendees leave with); host. Quarter plan: 1 session/month, formats varied. Backlog: topic, reuse value (how many BAs hit this), effort, suggested format.

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example session design: "Session: Interface specs teardown (case clinic). Why now: 3 programs writing interface specs, quality varying. Format: 2 real (de-identified) specs reviewed live against the checklist. Prep: authors volunteer, 10-min read. Takeaway: annotated exemplar spec for the knowledge base. Host: Senior Tech BA."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
