Master – Workforce & Resource Management

PL-04-M Practice Lead Workforce & Resource Management Master prompt 19/20 Library-ready

Manage BA demand, supply, recruitment and allocation across the portfolio: forecast demand, plan supply (hire/contract/develop), and prepare recruitment and onboarding materials. Produce on request

Who it is for

You are: a BA practice lead.
The audience for the output: Practice sponsor and finance (investment), PMO (allocation), HR (recruitment).

Target deliverables: Demand Forecasts, Resourcing Plans, Recruitment & Onboarding Materials

What you need to fill in

  • [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
  • [PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]
  • [KNOWN/LIKELY INITIATIVES AND TIMING]
  • [HEADCOUNT BY LEVEL, PERM/CONTRACT MIX, KNOWN DEPARTURES]

Before you paste anything in: My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding.

The prompt

CARE + G.E.T.

The library's native format. Context, Action, Result/Format, Example, plus Generate -> Explain -> Test. Lossless: every rubric criterion has somewhere to go.

# CONTEXT
I am a BA practice lead. I am working on [PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. My work must respect my organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything I paste is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert me before proceeding. Practice context: [PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]. Portfolio pipeline: [KNOWN/LIKELY INITIATIVES AND TIMING]. Current supply: [HEADCOUNT BY LEVEL, PERM/CONTRACT MIX, KNOWN DEPARTURES]. The audience for this output: Practice sponsor and finance (investment), PMO (allocation), HR (recruitment).

# ACTION
Manage BA demand, supply, recruitment and allocation across the portfolio: forecast demand, plan supply (hire/contract/develop), and prepare recruitment and onboarding materials. Produce on request:
1. demand forecast (FTE by level by month under committed/likely/possible scenarios, assumptions listed).
2. supply plan (current trajectory vs demand, gap analysis, response per gap with lead times and cost feel).
3. role descriptions (level, competencies from the framework, organisational context).
4. onboarding guide structure (week-by-week ramp, practice standards to learn, buddy model).
5. allocation principles when demand exceeds supply.

Before drafting, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions about anything missing, then wait for my answers.

# RESULT / FORMAT
Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Forecasts as scenarios (committed / likely / possible demand) not single lines; supply plans show lead times honestly (time-to-hire, time-to-productive); every gap has a named response (hire, contract, develop, decline work). Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience. Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from my inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE]. Treat the output as a first draft for my review, not a finished artefact.

# EXAMPLE
Example allocation principle: "When demand exceeds supply: regulatory-committed work first; then initiatives in delivery over discovery; discovery requests queue with a published wait time rather than silent thin-spreading (thin-spreading is how quality incidents happened in FY25)."

# VALIDATION (GENERATE -> EXPLAIN -> TEST)
Generate: produce the output requested above.
Explain: then break down, step by step, the logic you used to synthesise your answer - how you interpreted each of my inputs and why you structured the output the way you did.
Test: list the source references you used (my pasted inputs, earlier turns, or your general knowledge - label which is which); cross-reference the key facts in your output against those sources and flag anything that does not trace back to a source; and list every additional assumption you made so I can verify or correct it.

# Master – Workforce & Resource Management

Derived from `PL-04-M` in the BA Prompt Forge library
(Practice Lead / Workforce & Resource Management, scored 19/20
against the CARE + G.E.T. rubric).

## When to use this

The user is doing workforce & resource management work and needs one of:
- Demand Forecasts
- Resourcing Plans
- Recruitment & Onboarding Materials

The audience for whatever you produce is: **Practice sponsor and finance (investment), PMO (allocation), HR (recruitment)**.

## Before you start

The user's work must respect the user's organisation's privacy, information-security and data-residency obligations. Everything the user pastes is de-identified or synthetic — if any input appears to contain real personal or sensitive data, stop and alert them before proceeding.

Collect these inputs from the user. Do not guess them:

1. `[PROJECT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]`
2. `[PRACTICE SIZE, MIX OF PERMANENT/CONTRACT, DELIVERY PORTFOLIO SHAPE]`
3. `[KNOWN/LIKELY INITIATIVES AND TIMING]`
4. `[HEADCOUNT BY LEVEL, PERM/CONTRACT MIX, KNOWN DEPARTURES]`

Then ask up to 5 clarifying questions about anything still
missing, and **wait for the answers** before drafting.

## What to produce

- demand forecast (FTE by level by month under committed/likely/possible scenarios, assumptions listed)
- supply plan (current trajectory vs demand, gap analysis, response per gap with lead times and cost feel)
- role descriptions (level, competencies from the framework, organisational context)
- onboarding guide structure (week-by-week ramp, practice standards to learn, buddy model)
- allocation principles when demand exceeds supply

Format the output exactly as follows:

> Structured, evidence-based business analysis writing aligned to BABOK good practice. Use clear headings, numbered requirements and tables where they aid scanning. Plain English; expand every acronym on first use; no marketing language. Forecasts as scenarios (committed / likely / possible demand) not single lines; supply plans show lead times honestly (time-to-hire, time-to-productive); every gap has a named response (hire, contract, develop, decline work).

Keep the language professional, plain-English and appropriate for a business audience.

## Rules

- Do not invent facts or figures: mark anything you could not verify from the user's inputs as [TBC] and any value you propose yourself as [PROPOSED — VALIDATE].
- Treat the output as a first draft for the user's review, not a finished artefact.
- Do not ask the user to paste real personal or sensitive data.

## Worked example to imitate

Example allocation principle: "When demand exceeds supply: regulatory-committed work first; then initiatives in delivery over discovery; discovery requests queue with a published wait time rather than silent thin-spreading (thin-spreading is how quality incidents happened in FY25)."

## Before you hand it back

1. **Explain** - break down, step by step, the logic you used: how you interpreted
   each input and why you structured the output the way you did.
2. **Test** - list the sources you drew on (the user's inputs, earlier turns, or
   your own general knowledge; label which is which), cross-reference the key facts
   against them, flag anything that does not trace back to a source, and list every
   assumption you made so the user can verify or correct it.
